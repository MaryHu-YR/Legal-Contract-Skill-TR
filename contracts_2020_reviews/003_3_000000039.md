## Risk Analysis

### 🔴 Critical

**C-1. Bonus Determinator Has Unilateral, Unreviewable Discretion (§ 3)**
> "The determination of the satisfaction of the Bonus Conditions shall be made in writing by the Chairman of the Audit Committee of the Corporation's Board of Directors."

- **Issue:** A single corporate officer — not the full Board, not the Compensation Committee, not even a defined objective standard — decides whether the Executive gets a $250,000 payment already characterized as "awarded."
- **Risk:** If the Chairman of the Audit Committee withholds or delays the written determination for any reason (good-faith disagreement, personality conflict, change in Board composition), the Executive has no contractual remedy. The phrase "awarded" elsewhere in § 3 conflicts with the discretionary determination mechanism.
- **Market Standard:** Performance bonuses are normally governed by (a) objectively measurable targets, (b) joint certification by CEO/CFO/Compensation Committee, or (c) binding arbitration/independent expert if disputed. Single-officer discretion is unusual and disfavored.
- **Negotiability:** Medium — most public-company boards will accept adding an objective standard, a cure period, or an independent tie-breaker.
- **Redline:** Replace last sentence with: *"The Corporation shall promptly (and in any event no later than ten (10) business days following the satisfaction of the Bonus Conditions) deliver to the Executive a written certification of satisfaction signed by both the Chief Financial Officer and the Chairman of the Audit Committee. If the Executive disputes any such determination, the dispute shall be resolved by binding arbitration administered by JAMS in [venue] under its Employment Arbitration Rules."*

**C-2. Bonus Conditions Are Substantially Outside the Executive's Control (§ 3(b)–(c))**
> "(b) the Corporation's audited financial statements for the year ended September 30, 2020 shall have been completed and the Corporation's independent registered public accounting firm shall have issued an unqualified opinion on such financial statements, and (c) the Corporation shall have timely filed its Annual Report on Form 10-K..."

- **Issue:** Two of three bonus conditions depend on (i) auditor timing, (ii) auditor opinion type, and (iii) the Corporation's filing compliance — none of which the Executive (as co-CEO) can solely control. The "unqualified opinion" requirement is particularly aggressive: a "qualified" opinion (e.g., going-concern) — potentially arising from factors unrelated to Executive's performance — would forfeit the entire bonus.
- **Risk:** Real scenario: a going-concern qualification triggered by industry-wide CBD-market dislocation in late 2020 (the document was executed in November 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic) could deprive the Executive of the entire $250K even if he performed superbly. The "Agreement not terminated" condition in § 3(a) is also problematic: the Corporation could arguably terminate-for-cause the day before payment and avoid the bonus.
- **Market Standard:** Executive bonuses tied to **achievability and controllability**. Where bonuses reference accounting or filing milestones, market practice is to use a "good-faith determination by the Compensation Committee" or to add a "de minimis / immateriality" carve-out.
- **Negotiability:** Medium–High.
- **Redline (combined with C-1):** Add a clause: *"For the avoidance of doubt, conditions (b) and (c) shall be deemed satisfied if (x) the audited financial statements are issued and the auditor's opinion is unqualified, **or** (y) any qualification, explanatory paragraph, or delay arises from causes other than the Executive's gross negligence or willful misconduct, as determined by an independent accounting firm mutually selected by the parties."*

**C-3. Underlying Employment Agreement Not Reviewed (§ 4)**
> "Except as set forth herein, all other terms and conditions of the Sumichrast Employment Agreement remain in full force and effect."

- **Issue:** This amendment explicitly preserves and incorporates all terms of the September 6, 2018 Employment Agreement — a contract that is referenced but not provided. Critical provisions (termination for cause / convenience, severance, non-compete, IP assignment, confidentiality, restrictive covenants, change-in-control, dispute resolution, indemnification, governing law) are entirely opaque.
- **Risk:** The Executive could be signing an amendment that, read together with the 2018 Agreement, creates obligations or waives rights not visible in the four corners of this document. A 2018-vintage CBD-industry executive agreement also pre-dates the 2018 Farm Bill clarification of hemp-derived CBD legality and likely contains assumptions that may need updating.
- **Market Standard:** Diligence practice requires the full contract to be reviewed in connection with any amendment.
- **Negotiability:** N/A (procedural — not a clause negotiation).
- **Redline / Action:** Request and attach the September 6, 2018 Sumichrast Employment Agreement (including any prior amendments) as **Exhibit A** to this Amendment, with a recital confirming completeness.

### 🟡 Important

**I-1. Corporate-Governance / Signature-Authority Concern (Signature Block)**
- **Issue:** The CFO — not the Compensation Committee or independent directors — signs on behalf of the Corporation when the recipient is the Chairman/co-CEO.
- **Risk:** Self-dealing / interested-director transactions. Even where state law validates the contract (NCBCA § 55-8-30 et seq.), a disappointed stockholder or future plaintiff could attack the compensation as not properly authorized. The Executive also bears reputational risk.
- **Market Standard:** Public-company executive compensation is approved by the Compensation Committee (composed of independent directors), with disclosure in the proxy / 8-K where required.
- **Negotiability:** Low (governance is governance).
- **Redline / Action:** Have the Amendment **re-executed** by the Chair of the Compensation Committee (or, if no separate Compensation Committee, by a majority of the independent directors), with reference to the approving board resolution.

**I-2. Salary Cannot Be Decreased — No Inflation / Performance Flex (§ 2(i))**
- **Issue:** "may be increased, but not decreased" plus annual review is one-sided in the Executive's favor.
- **Risk:** From the Executive's view this is **favorable**; from the Corporation's view it is a moderate risk during downturns. Flagged here for completeness.
- **Market Standard:** Public-company executive base salary is typically subject to Compensation Committee discretion; floors are unusual but not unheard of.
- **Negotiability:** None needed from the Executive side.

**I-3. "Discretionary Bonus" vs. "Awarded" Inconsistency (§ 3)**
- **Issue:** The bonus is labeled "discretionary" in the heading and "awarded" in the body. The two characterizations have different legal consequences. "Discretionary" generally preserves unilateral non-payment rights; "awarded" suggests the obligation is fixed.
- **Risk:** Litigation risk over the meaning of "discretionary" vs. "awarded."
- **Market Standard:** Clear and consistent terminology.
- **Negotiability:** High.
- **Redline:** Replace "discretionary bonus" with "stay bonus" or "retention bonus," and remove any implication of discretion over payment once conditions are met.

**I-4. Termination of Underlying Agreement Forfeits Bonus (§ 3(a))**
- **Issue:** Bonus is forfeited if the Sumichrast Employment Agreement "has been terminated by either party for any reason" before payment.
- **Risk:** The Corporation could terminate-for-cause the day before payment and avoid the $250K. From the Executive's view, this is a forfeiture trap.
- **Market Standard:** "For any reason" is broader than typical. Market practice narrows this to "terminated for Cause" (defined term).
- **Negotiability:** High.
- **Redline:** Change "for any reason" → "for Cause (as defined in the Sumichrast Employment Agreement) or by the Executive without Good Reason."
