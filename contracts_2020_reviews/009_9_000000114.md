## Risk Analysis

### 🔴 Critical

**1. Equity Forfeiture Tied Exclusively to a Single Binary Regulatory Event (Tranche 1 & 2 Options)**

> "Any portion of the option associated with an incomplete performance milestone as of December 31, 2022 will automatically be forfeited and will be canceled."
> "If the NDA is not approved by December 31, 2022, these options will automatically be forfeited and will be canceled."

- **Issue:** 100% of Tranche 2 (and the unvested portion of Tranche 1) is contingent on **FDA approval of veverimer by 12/31/2022**. The FDA approval timeline is determined by an independent government agency, not by the executive. The executive bears 100% of the equity forfeiture risk for a regulatory event they cannot control.
- **Risk:** Even outstanding job performance cannot prevent forfeiture if the FDA delays or denies approval. With a closing-window hard date (12/31/2022) on a regulatory action, this is essentially a single-event bet.
- **Market Standard:** Retention equity typically uses (a) time-based vesting with a "good leaver" carve-out, (b) performance milestones the employee can influence (sales targets, development milestones), or (c) at minimum, a "personal performance" component so that the executive is rewarded for their work even if external events intervene.
- **Negotiability:** **Medium-High** — Company wants to retain through the approval; reasonable ask is to add personal performance component or extend the milestone deadline.
- **Redline:** Add: *"Notwithstanding the foregoing, if the NDA for veverimer has not been approved by December 31, 2022 due to (a) FDA action, request, or delay not caused by Executive's negligence, or (b) Company decision to delay, modify, or withdraw the NDA, then vesting shall continue on the original schedule with a [12]-month automatic extension, and the options shall remain outstanding until any such determination."* Also add: *"In the event of a Change of Control of the Company prior to December 31, 2022, the time-based milestones shall be deemed achieved immediately prior to closing."*

---

**2. "Good Standing" and "Due Diligence" Subjective Standards for Cash Retention**

> "You are in good standing with the Company on the installment date"
> "You with due diligence and good faith, fulfill your job responsibilities and meet your performance objectives both related to your present position and/or related to any position at the Company held by you in the future"

- **Issue:** "Good standing" and "due diligence" are undefined. The Company can unilaterally determine, in its sole discretion, that the executive is *not* in good standing (e.g., following a PIP, a performance review, an internal investigation, a dispute) and thereby deny the cash retention payment. There is no objective standard, no notice requirement, no cure period, and no appeal right.
- **Risk:** Executive could perform well, miss a cash installment on a technicality, and have no recourse. Particularly dangerous if a dispute arises with management in the period leading up to a payment.
- **Market Standard:** Objective performance standards (e.g., "not on active PIP," "not under investigation for cause," with written notice and a 30-day cure right).
- **Negotiability:** **High** — these are easily tightened with objective standards.
- **Redline:** Replace "good standing" with: *"'Good standing' means the Executive (i) is not on an active performance improvement plan, (ii) has not been given written notice of termination for cause, and (iii) has substantially performed the principal duties of their role. Any determination of lack of good standing shall be made in good faith, shall require written notice to the Executive specifying the basis, and shall be subject to a 30-day cure period."*

**3. No-Resignation Clause / Mandatory Continued Employment (Cash Retention T&Cs, Bullet 4)**

> "You do not resign your employment for any reason before the installment date."

- **Issue:** The executive is contractually barred from resigning — *for any reason* — before each installment date or they forfeit the entire cash retention payment. The agreement does not require the Company to provide notice, severance, or any consideration in return for this restriction. Combined with "at will" employment, this means the executive can be terminated by the Company for any reason (or no reason) and lose the cash, but cannot voluntarily leave without losing it.
- **Risk:** Asymmetric. The Company can fire the executive and not pay retention; the executive can be fired for cause and not be paid retention. Even a voluntary resignation for good cause (e.g., hostile work environment, demotion) results in forfeiture.
- **Market Standard:** Retention agreements typically allow resignation for "good reason" (defined) without forfeiture, and they provide severance to the employee if the Company terminates without cause during the retention window.
- **Negotiability:** **High** — fundamental to retention design.
- **Redline:** Replace bullet 4 with: *"You do not resign your employment without Good Reason (as defined below) before the installment date. 'Good Reason' shall mean, without the Executive's written consent, (a) a material reduction in title, authority, or reporting line; (b) a material reduction in base compensation; (c) a relocation of more than 50 miles; or (d) a material breach of this Agreement by the Company. The Executive shall provide written notice to the Company within 30 days of the event, and the Company shall have 30 days to cure."*

**4. No Severance, No Change-of-Control Acceleration, No "Good Leaver" Provisions**

- **Issue:** The document does not address what happens in (a) a Company-initiated termination without cause during the retention window, (b) a termination following a Change of Control (acquisition, merger), or (c) death, disability, or retirement. The default under the Cash Retention T&Cs is that any termination — for any reason — causes forfeiture. There is no acceleration, no pro-rata payment, no double-trigger protection.
- **Risk:** In an acquisition scenario, the acquiring company inherits the obligation but can simply terminate the executive (often with cause under a generous definition) and the retention evaporates. This is the most common and most devastating failure mode of executive retention packages.
- **Market Standard:** (a) Severance of 12–24 months base + target bonus on termination without cause during retention period; (b) double-trigger acceleration on Change of Control plus termination; (c) pro-rata vesting on death/disability; (d) "good leaver" carve-out preserving some portion of unvested awards.
- **Negotiability:** **High** — these are standard asks in any executive retention package.
- **Redline (Severance):** *"If the Company terminates the Executive's employment without Cause (as defined in the 2018 Plan) at any time prior to the final installment date, the Executive shall receive (i) continued payment of base salary for [12] months, (ii) pro-rata vesting of all equity awards scheduled to vest within [12] months following termination, and (iii) the Cash Retention Award installment(s) that would have become payable within [12] months following termination, payable on the original schedule."*
- **Redline (Change of Control):** *"Upon a Change of Control of the Company (as defined in the 2018 Plan), all outstanding unvested equity awards held by the Executive shall vest in full immediately prior to the closing of such transaction. In the event the Executive's employment is terminated by the Company without Cause or by the Executive for Good Reason within [12] months following a Change of Control, all remaining Cash Retention Award installments shall become payable within 30 days of termination."*
- **Redline (Death/Disability):** *"Upon the Executive's death or Disability (as defined in the 2018 Plan), all time-based equity awards shall vest in full and all Cash Retention Award installments that would have become payable within [12] months shall accelerate and be paid to the Executive or their estate."*

- **Negotiability:** **High**.

**7. No Tax Provisions (Section 409A, Section 280G, Section 162(m), Withholding)**

- **Market Standard:** 280G best-net cutback language (executive receives the better of (a) capped amount avoiding excise tax, or (b) full amount net of excise tax). 409A compliance language. Tax gross-up for required withholding beyond base amount.

## Missing Protections

### CRITICAL

**1. Change-of-Control Double-Trigger Acceleration**
- **Why it matters:** Most equity retention in pharma is destroyed in an acquisition when the acquirer terminates executives post-closing. Without CoC acceleration, the entire purpose of the retention can be lost in the most likely exit scenario.
- **What could go wrong:** Tricida is acquired in 2021, new management terminates executive within 6 months of closing, all unvested options and remaining cash retention installments are forfeited. The retention package that was sold to the executive as "long-term" was actually short-term.
- **Suggested clause:** *"Upon a Change of Control (as defined in the 2018 Plan), all outstanding unvested equity awards held by the Executive shall vest in full immediately prior to the closing of such transaction. In the event the Executive's employment is terminated by the Company without Cause or by the Executive for Good Reason within twelve (12) months following a Change of Control, all remaining Cash Retention Award installments shall be paid within thirty (30) days of such termination, and the Executive shall be entitled to severance equal to twelve (12) months of base salary plus target bonus."*

**2. Severance for Termination Without Cause During Retention Period**
- **Why it matters:** Without severance, the executive bears 100% of the termination risk. The Company can fire the executive at any time and avoid paying any retention.
- **What could go wrong:** Executive is terminated without cause in 2021 for a strategic reorganization. No severance paid, no retention, all unvested equity forfeited. Executive has no recourse.
- **Suggested clause:** *"If the Company terminates the Executive's employment without Cause at any time prior to the final installment date, the Executive shall receive (a) severance equal to twelve (12) months of base salary plus pro-rated target bonus, (b) pro-rata vesting of all equity awards scheduled to vest within the twelve (12) months following termination, and (c) any Cash Retention Award installment that would have become payable within the twelve (12) months following termination, payable on the original schedule. 'Cause' shall be defined as set forth in the 2018 Plan and shall require a written notice and 30-day cure period."*

**3. Definition of "Good Reason" with Carve-Out from No-Resignation Clause**
- **Why it matters:** Executive must have a meaningful ability to leave the Company in response to adverse changes. Without it, the no-resignation clause is coercive.
- **What could go wrong:** Executive's role is significantly diminished, title reduced, compensation cut, in a constructive discharge scenario — but the no-resignation clause forces them to stay to collect retention.
- **Suggested clause:** *"For purposes of the Cash Retention Award Terms and Conditions, the Executive's resignation for Good Reason shall not result in forfeiture. 'Good Reason' shall mean, without the Executive's written consent: (a) a material reduction in title, authority, reporting line, or scope of responsibility; (b) a reduction in base salary or target bonus opportunity; (c) a relocation of the Executive's principal workplace by more than fifty (50) miles; or (d) a material breach of this Agreement by the Company. The Executive shall provide written notice to the Company within thirty (30) days of the event, and the Company shall have thirty (30) days to cure."*

**4. Death and Disability Acceleration**
- **Why it matters:** Standard, expected, and absent.
- **Suggested clause:** *"Upon the Executive's death or Disability (as defined in the 2018 Plan), all time-based equity awards shall vest in full, all performance-based equity awards shall vest at target, and all Cash Retention Award installments scheduled to vest within twelve (12) months shall accelerate and be paid to the Executive or their estate within thirty (30) days."*
