### 🟡 Important

**1. No Governing Law / Controlling Document Reference (omission in the summary)**

- **Issue:** The exhibit does not specify which state's law governs, nor does it explicitly direct readers to the controlling 2020 Stock Incentive Plan, individual award agreements, or any director compensation policy.
- **Risk:** Ambiguity over which document controls in the event of a dispute; potential SEC disclosure risk if investors cannot locate the full framework.
- **Market Standard:** Public-company compensation summaries typically state "subject to the Company's 2020 Stock Incentive Plan" and reference the state of incorporation.
- **Negotiability:** High (Company controls drafting).
- **Suggested addition:** "These compensation arrangements are made under and subject to the Barrett Business Services, Inc. 2020 Stock Incentive Plan and are governed by the laws of the State of Oregon [or applicable state]."

**2. RSU Awards Concentrate Director Wealth in a Single Issuer (Director Perspective)**

- **Issue:** RSUs settle in BBSI common stock only. No deferred-compensation or stock-diversification alternative is offered.
- **Risk:** If BBSI underperforms, director realized compensation is depressed. From a Company-governance perspective, a single-stock concentration can also be cited by proxy advisory firms (ISS) as a board-independence concern.
- **Market Standard:** Many public companies (a) permit voluntary deferral of RSUs into a deferred-compensation plan, and/or (b) maintain minimum stock-ownership guidelines for directors.
- **Negotiability:** Medium-High for adding a deferral feature; low to add a stock-ownership guideline retroactively (typically prospective).
- **Suggested addition:** Adopt a director stock-ownership guideline (e.g., 3x annual cash retainer) and consider a non-qualified deferred compensation plan for RSUs.

**3. No Expense Reimbursement, D&O Insurance, or Indemnification Reference (Director Protection Gap)**

- **Issue:** The summary does not mention (a) reimbursement of reasonable out-of-pocket expenses incurred in board service, (b) D&O insurance coverage, or (c) indemnification.
- **Risk:** Although typically provided in the bylaws, charter, or indemnification agreements, a director reading only this summary would not see those protections. From a Company perspective, including a one-sentence cross-reference improves disclosure quality.
- **Market Standard:** A short sentence such as "Directors are reimbursed for reasonable expenses and are covered by the Company's D&O insurance and indemnification provisions" is typical.
- **Negotiability:** High.
- **Suggested addition:** "Each non-employee director is reimbursed for reasonable out-of-pocket expenses incurred in connection with board service and is entitled to indemnification and coverage under the Company's directors' and officers' liability insurance policy."
