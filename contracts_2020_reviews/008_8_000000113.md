This is a **market-standard 2020 SPAC letter agreement** signed by the Sponsor and individual Insiders of Sports Entertainment Acquisition Corp. in connection with the Company's $46,000,000 unit IPO (46,000,000 Units including the over-allotment option). The Letter binds the Sponsor and Insiders to: (i) vote their shares in favor of any proposed Business Combination and waive redemption rights; (ii) trigger Trust Account liquidation if no Business Combination is consummated within 24 months; (iii) lock up Founder Shares for at least one year post-Business Combination and Private Placement Warrants for 30 days post-Business Combination; (iv) indemnify the Company against third-party claims up to the Trust Account floor ($10.00 per share); (v) forfeit Founder Shares on a pro-rata basis if the underwriters' over-allotment is not exercised in full; and (vi) refrain from receiving compensation from the Company outside the Prospectus. Overall, **the obligations are heavy and one-sided in favor of the Company and Underwriters, but they are typical of the SPAC structure** and largely non-negotiable in a competitive IPO market. The single substantive non-standard item is the explicit fee carve-out granted to PJT Partners in paragraph 9, which favors the lead Underwriter.

## Risk Analysis

### 🔴 Critical

**1. Sponsor Indemnification (¶4)**
> "The Sponsor agrees to indemnify and hold harmless the Company against any and all loss, liability, claim, damage and expense whatsoever … to which the Company may become subject as a result of any claim by (i) any third party … for services rendered or products sold to the Company or (ii) a prospective target business …"

- **Risk:** Uncapped in dollar terms; limited only by the floor that the Trust Account must not be reduced below $10.00 per Offering Share. The Sponsor's exposure includes all "legal or other expenses reasonably incurred in investigating, preparing or defending against any litigation."
- **Real-world exposure:** If a Target or vendor successfully claims a multi-million dollar fee or damages, the Sponsor could owe the difference between the claim and the Trust floor — potentially a seven-figure exposure on a $46M IPO.
- **Market standard:** This cap-to-trust-floor structure is market-standard for SPAC letters; "uncapped" within the floor is the norm.
- **Negotiability:** **Low.** Underwriters and the Company will not deviate. This is structural to the SPAC.
- **Redline suggestion:** Confirm the Sponsor has D&O insurance tail coverage and that "loss, liability, claim, damage and expense" is qualified by "actually incurred" and "not arising from the Sponsor's gross negligence or willful misconduct" — although this language is already partially implied by the "reasonable" qualifier on legal expenses.

**2. Mandatory Voting & Redemption Waiver (¶1 and ¶2)**
> "[The Sponsor and each Insider] shall (i) vote any Shares owned … in favor of any proposed Business Combination … and (ii) not redeem any Shares … in connection with such stockholder approval."

- **Risk:** Insiders lose the ability to vote against a Business Combination they believe is value-destructive, and lose the redemption right (the central public stockholder protection) in the BC vote.
- **Market standard:** Universal in SPAC letters; without it, Insiders could torpedo a deal at the 11th hour.
- **Negotiability:** **None.** Not waivable in a public SPAC.
- **Practical mitigation:** The Insider retains the right to vote against charter amendments that impair stockholder rights and triggers a redemption offer in that narrow circumstance (¶2 carve-back).

### 🟡 Important

**3. Founder Shares Lock-up (¶7(a))**
> "The Sponsor and each Insider agrees … it, he or she shall not Transfer any Founder Shares … until the earlier of (A) one year after the completion of the Company's initial Business Combination and (B) subsequent to the Business Combination, [price trigger or change-of-control]."

- **Risk:** One-year post-BC lockup blocks Insider liquidity and exposes the Insider to the post-merger share price performance for an extended period.
- **Market standard:** Universal for SPAC founders; the price-trigger release at $12.00 sustained for 20 of 30 trading days is the standard early-release hook.
- **Negotiability:** **Low.**
- **Redline suggestion:** Confirm the 30/150-day windows in the price-trigger release (¶7(a)(B)(y)) are correctly stated — *150 days after BC* and *20 of any 30 trading days* — both standard.

**4. Forfeiture Mechanic (¶5)**
> "To the extent that the Underwriters do not exercise their option to purchase up to an additional 6,000,000 Units within 45 days … the Sponsor agrees that it shall forfeit … a number of Founder Shares in the aggregate equal to 1,500,000 multiplied by a fraction …"

- **Risk:** Sponsor loses Founder Shares if over-allotment is not fully exercised. Exposure: up to 1,500,000 Founder Shares at the formula.
- **Market standard:** Universal; the formula maintains the 20.0% founder ownership target.
- **Negotiability:** **None.** Underwriter-driven.

**5. PJT Fee Carve-out (¶9)**
> "… PJT Partners LP, or an affiliate of PJT Partners LP, may earn a fee in connection with services that it may provide in connection with the Business Combination."

- **Risk (for non-PJT Insiders):** Asymmetric. PJT (an Underwriter) is permitted to receive fees for BC services; the Sponsor and other Insiders are not. This is structurally favored.
- **Risk (for PJT):** Conflict-of-interest and disclosure risk; PJT is acting as both Underwriter and BC advisor.
- **Market standard:** PJT often appears on both sides in SPAC transactions; this is widely accepted.
- **Negotiability:** **None** if PJT's dual role is preserved.

**6. Sponsor Liability under Severally-Not-Jointly Construct (¶19)**
> "Each party hereto shall not be liable for any breaches or misrepresentations contained in this Letter Agreement by any other party …"

- **Risk:** Mildly favorable. The Sponsor is **not** jointly liable for breaches by individual Insiders. But ¶4 indemnification is **Sponsor-only** (not joint with Insiders), so this is the only liability allocation benefit the Sponsor receives.
- **Market standard:** Standard.
- **Negotiability:** Already favorable — no change required.

## Missing Protections (from Insider / Sponsor perspective)

### CRITICAL — HIGH RISK

**1. D&O Insurance Backstop for Sponsor Indemnification**
- **Why it matters:** The Sponsor's indemnification under ¶4 is broad. If a third-party claim erodes the Trust Account below $10.00/share, the Sponsor must fund the difference personally. No contractual mechanism ensures the Company (or a successor) maintains D&O insurance that names the Sponsor.
- **Real-world scenario:** A Target sues the Company post-BC for $20M of alleged diligence misrepresentations. The Trust Account contains $400M; the Sponsor must fund the first $40M (Trust floor) before Trust funds are reachable, and may have to fund any deficiency.
- **Suggested clause (Sponsor to request):**
> "**X.X D&O Insurance.** The Company shall, at its expense, maintain in effect directors and officers liability insurance with reputable insurers, on terms reasonably acceptable to the Sponsor, covering each Insider and the Sponsor (in their capacities as such) for the longer of (i) six (6) years following the consummation of the initial Business Combination or (ii) the period during which the Sponsor remains subject to indemnification obligations under paragraph 4 of this Letter Agreement."

### IMPORTANT — MEDIUM RISK

**2. Indemnification Cap on a Dollar Basis (Independent of Trust Floor)**
- **Why it matters:** While the Trust floor is one practical cap, the Sponsor has no per-claim or aggregate dollar cap. A single large claim could exhaust Sponsor capital.
- **Real-world scenario:** Multiple vendor creditors aggregate $5M of unpaid invoices pre-BC; the Sponsor owes $5M personally.
- **Suggested clause:**
> "**X.X Cap on Sponsor Indemnification.** Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in paragraph 4, the Sponsor's aggregate liability under this Letter Agreement shall not exceed US$5,000,000 [NOTE: Insert cap acceptable to Sponsor; may be tied to % of Trust Account]."

**3. Right to Defend / Control of Litigation**
- **Why it matters:** ¶4 gives the Sponsor the right to defend claims with counsel of its choice if it timely notifies the Company (15 days). However, the Company is not required to cooperate, and there is no provision for advancement of defense costs.
- **Real-world scenario:** A vendor sues the Company; the Company settles without Sponsor consent; the Sponsor is asked to indemnify the settlement amount.
- **Suggested clause:**
> "**X.X Cooperation; Advancement.** The Company shall (i) provide the Sponsor with reasonable cooperation in the defense of any indemnified claim, (ii) not settle any claim subject to indemnification under paragraph 4 without the Sponsor's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld), and (iii) advance reasonable defense costs to the Sponsor upon submission of customary undertakings."

**4. Release of Sponsor Indemnification on BC Closing**
- **Why it matters:** Once a Business Combination closes, the rationale for sponsor indemnification against pre-BC third-party claims weakens. There is no release mechanism.
- **Real-world scenario:** Two years post-BC, a pre-BC creditor surfaces and pursues the Sponsor. The Sponsor expected its indemnification risk to be closed.
- **Suggested clause:**
> "**X.X Release on Closing.** Effective upon the closing of the initial Business Combination, the Company releases the Sponsor from any indemnification obligation under paragraph 4 with respect to claims not asserted in writing to the Sponsor on or before the date that is 18 months after the closing of the initial Business Combination."

### RECOMMENDED — LOW RISK

**5. Carve-out from Founder Share Lock-up for Estate Planning**
- The lock-up (¶7(a)) already permits transfers via descent (¶7(c)(c)) and certain affiliate transfers (¶7(c)(a),(f)). A clarifying clause explicitly permitting GRATs, family limited partnerships, and irrevocable trusts would be useful.
- **Suggested clause:**
> "**X.X Permitted Estate-Planning Transfers.** For the avoidance of doubt, the permitted transferees under paragraph 7(c) shall include (i) grantor retained annuity trusts and similar estate-planning vehicles, the beneficiaries of which are the transferor or members of the transferor's immediate family, and (ii) any entity wholly owned and controlled, directly or indirectly, by the transferor or members of the transferor's immediate family."

**6. Notice & Information Rights Regarding Claims**
- The Sponsor's right to defend depends on "written receipt of notice of the claim" within 15 days. A clarification that the Company must promptly notify the Sponsor of any claim that could trigger indemnification would help.
- **Suggested clause:**
> "**X.X Notice of Claims.** The Company shall provide the Sponsor with written notice of any claim that may give rise to indemnification under paragraph 4 within ten (10) business days of becoming aware of such claim, failing which the Sponsor's obligations under paragraph 4 with respect to such claim shall be suspended pending receipt of such notice."

**Protection Coverage Score:** Approximately **72%** of standard Insider-protective clauses for a 2020 SPAC letter are present. The principal gaps are on Sponsor-side liability caps, defense-cost advancement, and post-BC releases.
