## Risk Analysis

### 🔴 Critical

**C-1. Permanent waiver of all other at-risk incentive or bonus compensation (¶ 5)**

> "…you hereby acknowledge and agree that, notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in section 2.2 of your Employment Agreement, you shall not be entitled to and you hereby waive any additional annual at risk incentive or bonus amounts beyond the entitlement to the CaPre Retention Incentive…"

- **Issue:** This is a one-way waiver that surrenders Mr. Lemieux's right to *any* future annual bonus, even after the CaPre Retention Incentive has been paid or forfeited. The only thing preserved is the existing severance terms of the 2017 Employment Agreement.
- **Why it matters:** The 2017 Employment Agreement likely entitles the executive to an annual discretionary or target bonus. By signing, that right is extinguished going forward — possibly for the rest of his tenure — in exchange for a single conditional payment of at most US$125K.
- **Market standard:** Retention awards are usually *additive* to, not *in lieu of*, the executive's existing compensation architecture.
- **Negotiability (had it been pre-execution):** Medium-high; this is the single largest give-up.
- **Practical concern post-execution:** The waiver is contractually binding; remedy is interpretive only (e.g., argue the waiver was limited to a defined period or specific program).

**C-2. "Voluntary termination" and "for cause" are undefined (¶ 4)**

> "Should you voluntarily terminate your employment with the Company or should your employment be terminated by the Company for cause…you will forfeit any entitlement…"

- **Issue:** No definition of (i) what constitutes a "voluntary" resignation (constructive dismissal? mutual separation? garden leave?) or (ii) what "for cause" means (gross misconduct only? poor performance?).
- **Why it matters:** A Canadian employer could attempt to characterize a constructive-dismissal situation (e.g., a significant demotion, relocation, or pay cut) as a "voluntary" resignation — defeating the incentive. The same ambiguity exists in reverse: "for cause" under Quebec law has a high threshold, but the contract does not import that standard.
- **Market standard:** "Cause" is typically defined in writing and includes a list of enumerated events plus a procedural/cure mechanism.
- **Negotiability:** High — definitions are routine asks.

**C-3. No outer sunset / long-stop date (¶ 3)**

- **Issue:** The triggers (a)–(c) can occur "prior to or in connection with" a Merger Transaction, with no specified deadline. If the Company indefinitely delays monetization, abandons CaPre, or simply never pursues a Merger Transaction, the Employee waits without recourse.
- **Why it matters:** A retention bonus without a sunset is a unilateral option held by the Company: it can pay if convenient and decline to monetize if not.
- **Market standard:** Retention bonuses typically include a maximum service period (e.g., payable on the earlier of trigger X or December 31, 2023), with a fallback if the Company materially changes direction.
- **Negotiability:** Medium — most issuers accept a 24–36 month sunset.

**C-4. Broad confidentiality of the offer's existence (¶ 8)**

> "…the content and existence of this offer shall remain confidential and shall not be disclosed to any third party without the prior written consent of the Company."

- **Issue:** The Employee cannot tell a spouse, financial advisor, attorney, accountant, or prospective employer that the offer exists without the Company's prior written consent.
- **Why it matters:** Limits the Employee's ability to seek professional advice, plan personal finances, or negotiate with recruiters. While most NDAs allow disclosure to "Representatives" (legal, tax, financial advisors) on a need-to-know basis, this one does not.
- **Market standard:** Confidentiality clauses always carve out advisors, accountants, attorneys, and tax authorities.
- **Negotiability:** High — the "Representatives" carve-out is a routine ask.

**C-5. No dispute resolution mechanism (omitted)**

- **Issue:** No mediation, arbitration, or pre-litigation process. Disputes go straight to Quebec courts under Quebec procedural law.
- **Why it matters:** Quebec courts are appropriate, but a senior executive often benefits from confidential arbitration (e.g., ADRIC / ADR Institute of Canada) to keep disputes out of public record.
- **Market standard:** Medium and large deals often include a tiered escalation (negotiation → mediation → arbitration).
- **Negotiability:** Medium.

### 🟡 Important

**I-1. Single-trigger vs. double-trigger treatment is unclear (¶ 3)**

- **Issue:** Whether the CaPre Retention Incentive *vests* on the announcement of a Merger Transaction, on its closing, or only on the Employee remaining employed at closing is ambiguous. The letter says "earned and payable only upon the first to occur of the following events," but does not say whether the Employee must still be employed at that moment.
- **Why it matters:** If Mr. Lemieux is terminated *without cause* between signing and closing, he gets only the Minimum ($75K). If he is still employed at closing, he may receive more — but the letter is not explicit.
- **Redline (recommended):** Add: "The Employee must be actively employed by the Company on the date the applicable trigger under (a), (b), or (c) occurs, except in the case of a termination by the Company without Cause, in which case the Minimum CaPre Retention Incentive shall be payable as set forth above."

**I-2. "Pre-Merger CaPre Monetization" includes reduction of payables (¶ 3(a))**

- **Issue:** "Including any reduction of payables relating to the CaPre Assets" effectively allows the Company to *not paying its own bills* to count as "monetization." This is a back-door mechanism to manufacture a trigger.
- **Why it matters:** If Acasti simply delays paying vendors/suppliers, gross-proceeds-equivalent accounting could push the Employee into a higher tier.
- **Redline (recommended):** Exclude "reduction of payables" from the definition, or limit it to bona fide negotiated settlements above book value.

**I-3. One-sided authority over monetization strategy (¶ 3(a))**

- **Issue:** The Company controls when, whether, and how to monetize CaPre Assets. The Employee has no voice and no remedy if the Company decides not to pursue monetization or to assign CaPre to a non-arm's-length entity at a low value.
- **Redline (recommended):** Add an "Efforts to Monetize" covenant: the Company shall use commercially reasonable efforts to monetize the CaPre Assets on arm's-length terms prior to the consummation of a Merger Transaction.

**I-4. Acknowledgement of severance is helpful but limited (¶ 5)**

- **Issue:** The letter helpfully *reaffirms* the existing severance entitlements under the 2017 Employment Agreement (30 days' notice + 12 months base salary + accrued vacation + benefits). However, the express waiver of additional at-risk compensation may interact with section 2.2 in ways that a Quebec court might interpret against the Employee.
- **Recommendation:** Cross-reference the entire 2017 Employment Agreement and add a savings clause: "Nothing in this offer shall limit, modify, or terminate any right of the Employee under the Employment Agreement other than the annual at-risk incentive as expressly waived herein."

**I-5. No tax protection or section 280G treatment (omitted)**

- **Issue:** In a Canadian context, paragraph 6801 of the Income Tax Act and Quebec tax rules may treat retention bonuses as either employment income or a retiring allowance. The letter does not address source deductions, withholdings, or 280G-equivalent excise tax in a U.S. context.
- **Note:** This is more relevant to the Company; the Employee should at least understand the gross-vs-net impact (the letter says "less applicable withholding taxes" but does not define gross-up).

**I-6. No mitigation or offset language (omitted)**

- **Issue:** If the Employee is paid the Minimum CaPre Retention Incentive on a without-cause termination, there is no language addressing whether that amount offsets severance or any other compensation. A strict reading could allow the Company to argue double recovery.
- **Redline (recommended):** "The Minimum CaPre Retention Incentive is in addition to, and not in lieu of, any severance or termination pay owed to the Employee under the Employment Agreement or applicable law."

**I-7. No "good leaver / bad leaver" distinctions (¶ 4)**

- **Issue:** A "good leaver" (e.g., death, disability, retirement) is not separately addressed. The current language only addresses voluntary, for-cause, and without-cause.
- **Redline (recommended):** Add: "In the event of the Employee's death or Disability (as defined in the Employment Agreement), the Minimum CaPre Retention Incentive shall be earned and payable to the Employee or his estate, as applicable."

## Recommended Next Steps

1. **Obtain a copy of the 2017 Employment Agreement** and review section 2.2 to understand exactly what was waived. If section 2.2 includes change-of-control, transaction-bonus, or retention rights, those are now gone.
2. **Engage Quebec-licensed counsel** to assess whether the waiver in ¶ 5 is enforceable under the Quebec *Charte des droits et libertés de la personne* and the Quebec *Civil Code* (arts. 1437–1438 on abusive clauses in adhesion contracts). Senior-executive contracts are generally *not* adhesion contracts, but a Quebec court could examine proportionality.
3. **Document the Company's monetization efforts** going forward. Request the "commercially reasonable efforts" language in writing (or, if a successor retention agreement is being negotiated, add it as a condition).
4. **Track the trigger events** (a), (b), (c) carefully. Maintain a personal record of any communications about CaPre Asset sales, NOL transfers, CVR structures, or Merger Transactions.
5. **Negotiate a side letter** clarifying (i) the duration of the at-risk bonus waiver, (ii) the continued-employment requirement at trigger, and (iii) the inclusion of "Representatives" in the confidentiality exception. Even executed retention letters can sometimes be clarified by contemporaneous side letter or amendment.
6. **Tax planning.** The letter says "less applicable withholding taxes." Confirm with a Canadian tax advisor whether any portion can be structured as a retiring allowance (and rolled to a Registered Retirement Savings Plan under paragraph 60(j.1) of the *Income Tax Act*) or whether it must be ordinary employment income.
7. **Plan for the worst case.** If the Company is acquired or merges, confirm the "Merger Transaction" definition is satisfied, then confirm whether the Employee is still employed at closing.
8. **Run a Legal Compare or Negotiate workflow** if you have a draft successor agreement; the issues above can be carried into a new retention framework.
