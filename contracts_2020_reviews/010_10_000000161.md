## Executive Summary

This is a **§423-qualified ESPP** that allows eligible employees to acquire IPG common stock at a 90% discount to the lesser of the beginning- or end-of-period price (a "lookback"). The plan uses 3-month Purchase Periods (vs. the more common 6-month) with a 10% payroll-deduction cap, a $25,000 annual statutory limit, and a 5% stockholder exclusion.

For an employee, the **90% lookback is the standout employee-favorable feature** — it allows purchases below the market low if the stock falls during the period. The **chief concerns** are: (i) "Compensation" excludes bonuses, overtime, and most variable pay, capping the dollar value employees can contribute; (ii) very broad amendment authority vested in the General Counsel, with unreviewable "material financial impact" determinations; (iii) deemed termination if a participating subsidiary exits the corporate group, with a complete loss of accumulated payroll deductions; and (iv) the Corporation's right to use payroll-deduction funds for general corporate purposes (no segregated trust).

**Recommendation:** Acceptable plan to participate in, but read the discretionary provisions carefully, do not over-allocate compensation in light of the bonus exclusion, and consider whether an open-market purchase through your 401(k) provides more flexibility for variable compensation.

## Risk Analysis

### 🔴 Critical (from employee perspective)

#### C1. "Compensation" Excludes Bonuses, Overtime, and Most Variable Pay — §11

> "*Compensation* means only basic compensation, including any elective deferral … and any salary reduction under a cafeteria plan …, **and excluding overtime, bonuses, cost-of-living allowances, deferred compensation awards** … or any other extra payment of any kind …"

- **Issue:** The 10% deduction cap (§5) and the share-purchase formula (§8(a)) both reference "Compensation." Excluding bonuses means a large portion of pay for many IPG employees (especially in ad-agency roles with significant bonus/incentive comp) cannot be used to purchase stock.
- **Real-world impact:** An employee earning $100K base + $50K bonus = $150K total comp — only $10K (10% of $100K) can go to ESPP. Industry standard now includes at least target bonus.
- **Market standard:** Major U.S. ESPPs typically include base + bonus + commissions, or at least target bonus. Pure-base-only definitions are increasingly rare.
- **Negotiability:** **None** for an individual employee. (This is a Board-level plan design decision.) Practical workaround: allocate to 401(k) the variable comp you cannot allocate to ESPP.
- **Risk Score:** 7/10

#### C2. Subsidiary Exit / Inter-Subsidiary Transfer Triggers Deemed Termination — §13

> "If, prior to the last business day of the Plan Period, the participating Subsidiary by which an employee is employed shall cease to be a subsidiary of the Corporation, or if the employee is transferred to a subsidiary of the Corporation that is not a participating Subsidiary, **the employee shall be deemed to have terminated employment for the purposes of this Plan**."

- **Issue:** A complete forfeiture of accumulated payroll deductions (refunded in cash) and loss of any in-period option, even if the divestiture or transfer has nothing to do with employee performance.
- **Real-world scenario:** IPG divests a non-core agency; you lose 2.5 months of accumulated deductions at the end of a Purchase Period. For a $150K employee contributing the max, that could be ~$3,750 in accumulated cash.
- **Market standard:** Some plans preserve the option or convert to a parallel cash payment on a §423 "designated company" event. This plan does not.
- **Negotiability:** None for individual employees. The MHRC could be petitioned to add a "qualifying corporate event" carve-out, but adoption is at its discretion.
- **Risk Score:** 7/10

#### C3. Unreviewable "MFI" Determinations — §11, §17

> "… the determination as to whether an amendment has or potentially has a positive or negative material financial impact shall be made by the MHRC or the General Counsel of the Corporation, **in its or his sole discretion, and shall not be subject to challenge or question by any person** …"

- **Issue:** Amendments that materially affect employee economics (e.g., changing the discount, changing the share cap, freezing the plan) can be made by the General Counsel without Board action, *provided* the General Counsel opines the change does not have a Material Financial Impact ("MFI"). The MFI definition is itself unreviewable.
- **What you could lose:** The 90% discount, the lookback feature, share caps, and the "Compensation" definition are all amendable under §17. Only changes to *total share reserve* and *purchase price* require shareholder approval.
- **Negotiability:** None at the individual level. Watch IPG's 8-Ks and proxy statements for plan amendments.
- **Risk Score:** 7/10

## Missing Protections

### CRITICAL (HIGH RISK)

#### M1. Inclusion of Bonus / Variable Compensation in "Compensation"

**Why this matters:** A pure-base-salary definition is increasingly out-of-market. It artificially caps the dollar value employees can deploy to acquire discounted IPG stock. Since IPG's workforce is heavily incentive-compensated, this materially affects realized participation.

**Real-world scenario:** A senior strategist earning $200K base + $100K target bonus. Under the current definition, max ESPP contribution is $20K/year. A bonus-inclusive plan would allow $30K/year, an extra $10K of pre-tax stock acquisition per year × 5 years = $50K+ of additional compounding at the 90% discount.

**Suggested clause to add** (insert in §11 "Compensation" definition):

> "**Compensation** means base salary, wages, shift differentials, and any cash bonus or incentive compensation (including amounts that would be received but for an election to defer under a qualified plan or a cafeteria plan). Compensation **excludes** overtime premium pay, employer contributions to retirement or welfare plans, stock compensation income, severance, and imputed income."

#### M2. Successor-Plan / Qualifying Corporate Event Carve-out (Subsidiary Divestiture)

**Why this matters:** §13 currently treats a corporate transaction (subsidiary leaving the group) identically to a voluntary resignation. The employee loses the right to purchase at the lookback price despite having made an economic commitment through payroll deductions.

**Suggested clause to add** (new §13(c) or amendment to §13):

> "**(c) Qualifying Corporate Event.** If, during a Purchase Period, (i) the participating Subsidiary by which an employee is employed ceases to be a Subsidiary as a result of a merger, sale of assets, or other corporate transaction in which the Corporation does not retain at least 50% of the equity, or (ii) the Corporation enters into an agreement the consummation of which will result in a Change in Control, then the affected employee's Purchase Period shall be shortened and the option shall be automatically exercised on the latest practicable date before the closing (using the Average Market Price on such date), and the employee's accumulated cash balance shall be applied to purchase shares. Any cash remaining after such purchase shall be refunded to the employee."

#### M3. Anti-Cutback / Acquired Rights Limitation on Amendments

**Why this matters:** §17 allows broad amendment. While shareholder approval is required to change the share reserve or the per-share price, nothing prevents the Board (or General Counsel) from amending the discount, the lookback, or the eligibility rules mid-Period to the detriment of in-flight participants.

**Suggested clause to add** (insert at end of §17):

> "**No Impairment of In-Flight Options.** Notwithstanding any other provision of this Section 17, no amendment, suspension, or termination of the Plan shall impair the rights of any employee with respect to an option previously granted for the then-current Purchase Period, without the affected employee's written consent. For the avoidance of doubt, an in-flight option may be exercised, accelerated, or cashed out on a qualifying corporate event under §13(c), but may not be eliminated or made economically less favorable to the employee."

### IMPORTANT (MEDIUM RISK)

#### M4. Dispute Resolution / Claims Procedure

**Why this matters:** No mechanism is specified for an employee to challenge a denied enrollment, a denied withdrawal, an unfavorable purchase-price calculation, or a discretionary eligibility decision. ERISA-covered plans normally require a claims procedure; pure §423 ESPPs are typically **not** ERISA-covered (they're "bonus" plans), but employees still need a remedy.

**Suggested clause to add** (new §20(j)):

> "**(j) Administrative Review.** Any employee affected by a determination of the Committee or its delegate may, within 30 days of receiving written notice of the determination, request in writing a review by the MHRC. The MHRC shall respond in writing within 60 days. The MHRC's decision shall be final and binding on the employee and the Corporation."

#### M5. Insider Trading / Blackout Coordination

**Why this matters:** §8 purchases occur automatically on the last business day of the Purchase Period without reference to insider trading windows. A §16 officer receiving material non-public information could be forced to purchase shares on an auto-enrollment date, creating 10b-5 / §16(b) exposure.

**Suggested clause to add** (new §20(k)):

> "**(k) Trading Window Compliance.** If the last business day of a Purchase Period falls within a trading blackout period applicable to an employee under the Corporation's insider-trading policy, the option of any such affected employee shall be deemed automatically exercised on the first trading day after the blackout period ends, using the Average Market Price on such date."

#### M6. Account Statements / Electronic Access

**Why this matters:** §8 only requires a statement "promptly" after each purchase. Modern best practice is quarterly electronic statements showing contributions, share balances, and cost basis.

**Suggested clause to add** (new §20(l)):

> "**(l) Statements.** The Corporation shall provide each participating employee with at least an annual written (or electronic) statement showing contributions made, shares purchased (with dates and prices), total shares held, and estimated current value."
