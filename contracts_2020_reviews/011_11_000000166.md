## Risk Analysis

### 🔴 Critical

**1. Cash Settlement on Change in Control — § 2.5(b)**
> "RSUs that Vest on a Change in Control Date will be settled in cash in lieu of Shares, with the settlement value of each RSU calculated as the Fair Market Value of a Share on the Change in Control Date."

- **Issue:** Every other provision contemplates share settlement; the CIC path is the only place where the corporation pays cash. This creates a **mismatch in economic exposure** and a **§ 409A risk**: cash payments tied to a CIC that does not qualify as a "change in control event" under Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-3(a)(5) would fail the short-term-deferral exemption the rest of the agreement relies upon.
- **What you could lose:** A CIC is often when the stock is most valuable; receiving cash rather than shares strips the holder of any post-CIC appreciation and removes the ability to roll the position over tax-efficiently in a stock-for-stock deal.
- **Market Standard:** Public-company director RSUs almost always settle in **shares** on CIC. Cash settlement is more typical of performance-based or deferred compensation plans under § 409A.
- **Negotiability:** **Medium.** Sponsors of public-company plans often insist on share settlement on CIC; reasonable to ask for parity.
- **Redline:** Replace § 2.5(b) with share settlement consistent with § 2.5(a); alternatively, if cash is non-negotiable, request that the "Change in Control Date" be expressly defined to track the § 409A safe-harbor definition.

**2. Blank Fields in Executable Document — Page 1**
- **Issue:** "Number of RSUs," "Initial Value," and "Participant" are blank. The agreement is not legally complete; signing it without these values leaves the grant indeterminate.
- **Market Standard:** All economic terms must be filled in **before** execution; the Plan and the agreement operate together as a single instrument.
- **Negotiability:** **N/A** — this is a precondition to signing.
- **Redline:** Confirm the participant's name, the precise number of RSUs, and the corresponding dollar value (and closing-share-price reference) before signing.

### 🟡 Important

**3. Broad "Interruption or Termination" Forfeiture — § 2.3**
> "...in the event that Participant's Continuous Service is interrupted or terminated, all Restricted Shares not previously Vested will be forfeited immediately..."

- **Issue:** "Interrupted" is undefined in the agreement and is broader than the customary "terminated" language. Because the Restriction Period is only 12 months and the vesting is a single cliff, a director who misses a single board meeting or has a leave of absence could be at risk.
- **What you could lose:** The entire grant if the corporation characterizes a brief absence as an "interruption."
- **Market Standard:** "Continuous Service" is a defined term in the Plan (not reproduced here) and typically means the absence of a severance event. Forfeiture language should be tied to actual termination, not interruption.
- **Negotiability:** **Medium-High.** Most plan administrators will accept a clarification.
- **Redline:** Limit forfeiture to "termination of Continuous Service for any reason" and remove the word "interrupted"; or, alternatively, ensure the Plan's "Continuous Service" definition excludes brief leaves of absence.

**4. Mandatory Binding Arbitration — § 7**
> "...must be resolved by mandatory arbitration administered by and in accordance with the then effective arbitration rules of Arbitration Service of Portland, Inc."

- **Issue:** Mandatory arbitration is binding and waives the participant's right to a jury trial. The provision is one-sided in substance (the corporation chose the forum and the rules body), though neutral as to parties. There is no opt-out for claims that must by law be brought in court (e.g., § 16 short-swing claims) and no carve-out for injunctive relief needed to preserve the status quo.
- **Market Standard:** Many public companies require arbitration of director compensation disputes; however, the **American Bar Association and the Council of Institutional Investors** recommend carve-outs for injunctive relief and for claims that cannot legally be arbitrated.
- **Negotiability:** **Low-Medium.** The corporation is unlikely to abandon arbitration entirely; carve-outs are the realistic ask.
- **Redline:** Add: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, either party may seek injunctive or other equitable relief in court to prevent irreparable harm. Nothing herein shall be construed to waive any right or claim that, by law, cannot be subjected to mandatory arbitration."

**5. Thin Tax Reimbursement Provision — § 3**
> "In the event any withholding or similar tax liability is imposed on Corporation in connection with or with respect to any Vesting of the RSUs, Participant agrees to pay to Corporation an amount sufficient to provide for such tax liability."

- **Issue:** The agreement obligates the director to pay the tax liability but does **not** specify the corporation's right to **withhold shares or cash** to satisfy that obligation. This is also silent on the timing and the form of payment.
- **What you could lose:** Confusion at vesting — director may be expected to wire funds on the same day shares vest.
- **Market Standard:** Modern RSU agreements expressly authorize the corporation to withhold a number of vested shares (or sell shares) to satisfy tax withholding (a "net settlement" or "share-withholding" mechanism).
- **Negotiability:** **High.** A clarifying amendment is standard.
- **Redline:** Add: "Corporation is authorized to withhold from the Shares otherwise deliverable to Participant upon settlement a number of Shares with a Fair Market Value equal to the minimum statutory withholding obligation, or to require Participant to deliver cash or a check."

**6. No Dividend Equivalents — § 2.8 (acknowledged but not granted)**
- **Issue:** The agreement explicitly disclaims stockholder rights prior to issuance. RSUs in many public-company plans carry **dividend equivalent** rights that accrue during the restriction period.
- **What you could lose:** Any dividends declared during the 12-month restriction period on the underlying share count.
- **Market Standard:** Increasingly common in director plans; not universal.
- **Negotiability:** **Medium.** Reasonably common ask.
- **Redline:** Add: "Participant shall be entitled to dividend equivalents equal to any cash dividends that would have been paid on the RSUs, payable (with interest or at fair market value) at the time the underlying RSUs settle."

## Missing Protections

### CRITICAL — HIGH RISK

**1. Completed economic terms (Page 1)**
- **Why it matters:** An RSU agreement with blank number of units or value is unenforceable on its face.
- **What could go wrong:** A later dispute about what was actually granted; potential tax-reporting errors; an unintentional default vesting tied to "0" units.
- **Suggested language:**
  > "**Number of RSUs:** [●]
  > **Initial Value of Grant:** $[●], based on the closing price of a Share of Common Stock, $[●], on July 1, 2020.
  > **Participant:** [Full Legal Name]"

**2. Section 409A — defined "Change in Control Date"**
- **Why it matters:** § 10 relies on the short-term-deferral exemption, but § 2.5(b) introduces a cash payment keyed to "Change in Control Date." If that term is not defined to track Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-3(a)(5), the exemption collapses.
- **Suggested language:** Add a definition in § 1:
  > "**Change in Control Date** means the date on which a 'change in control event' (within the meaning of Treas. Reg. § 1.409A-3(a)(5)(i)) occurs, as determined in accordance with Section 409A of the Code."

**3. Section 280G excise-tax protection**
- **Why it matters:** CIC payments can trigger the 20% golden-parachute excise tax under § 280G; without a gross-up or safe-harvest cap, the director bears the tax personally.
- **Suggested language:**
  > "**X. Section 280G.** Notwithstanding any other provision of this Agreement, in the event that any payment or benefit to Participant under this Agreement, when combined with any other payment or benefit, would constitute a 'parachute payment' within the meaning of Section 280G of the Code, Corporation shall use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain shareholder approval in the manner permitted by Q-7 of Treas. Reg. § 1.280G-1 to avoid the imposition of the excise tax under Section 4999 of the Code."

### IMPORTANT — MEDIUM RISK

**4. Tax-withholding mechanism**
- **Why it matters:** Without explicit share-withholding authority, the corporation may require a same-day cash payment.
- **Suggested language:**
  > "**X. Tax Withholding.** Corporation shall be entitled to (a) withhold from any payment due to Participant, or (b) reduce the number of Shares otherwise deliverable upon settlement, in either case in an amount sufficient to satisfy any federal, state, local, or foreign tax withholding obligations arising in connection with the RSUs. Participant may, in the alternative, deliver to Corporation cash or a check equal to the required withholding amount."

**5. Reasonable "Continuous Service" carve-out**
- **Why it matters:** A one-day absence could be argued to "interrupt" service.
- **Suggested language:**
  > "For the avoidance of doubt, 'Continuous Service' shall not be deemed to be interrupted by reason of (i) any leave of absence approved by the Board, (ii) any temporary vacancy on the Board, or (iii) any other absence that does not constitute a 'separation from service' within the meaning of Section 409A."

**6. Dividend equivalents**
- **Suggested language:**
  > "**X. Dividend Equivalents.** Participant shall be entitled to receive, on each date that a cash dividend is paid on a Share of Common Stock during the Restriction Period, a cash amount equal to the product of (a) the per-share dividend and (b) the number of RSUs held by Participant on the dividend record date, payable at the time the underlying RSUs are settled."
