## Executive Summary

This is a **market-standard SPAC trust agreement** typical for blank-check companies going public. The agreement establishes a Trust Account at a major U.S. bank (Bank of America) holding IPO proceeds and private placement proceeds, restricting investments to short-term U.S. Treasury obligations and qualifying money market funds, and providing for liquidation upon either a Business Combination or the 24-month deadline (extendable by stockholder vote).

**Key takeaways:**
- The Trustee's role is **deliberately passive** — it acts only on the Company's written instructions and has no discretion over investment choices, Business Combination approval, or distribution logic.
- The Trustee has **waived any claim against the Trust Account** (Section 4) — a critical and very favorable protection for Public Stockholders and the Company.
- Liability of the Trustee is **narrowly limited** to gross negligence, fraud, or willful misconduct — standard in the industry.
- The **redemption mechanics** (Sections 1(k) and Exhibit D) and **tax withdrawal mechanics** (Section 1(j) and Exhibit C) follow SEC and stock exchange requirements.
- The **Underwriter is a third-party beneficiary** (Section 6(j)) and has priority for the Deferred Discount before any other distribution.

Overall, this is a well-understood, regulator-tested form. From the Company's perspective, it is acceptable and not unusual. There are no critical risk issues, but a few items (no force majeure, no explicit dispute resolution beyond litigation, broad Company indemnification of Trustee) warrant attention.

### 🟡 Important

**1. Broad Company Indemnification of Trustee (Section 2(b))**
> "hold the Trustee harmless and indemnify the Trustee from and against any and all reasonable and documented expenses, including reasonable counsel fees and disbursements, or losses suffered by the Trustee in connection with any action taken by it hereunder…"

- **Issue:** The Company's indemnity to the Trustee is uncapped, with the only carve-out being the Trustee's gross negligence, fraud, or willful misconduct.
- **Why it matters:** In a worst-case scenario (e.g., protracted litigation over the Trust Account, tax disputes, third-party claims related to distributions), the Company could face uncapped indemnification exposure to the Trustee.
- **Market standard:** This is **standard** for SPAC trust agreements; trustees will not accept capped indemnity because their role involves substantial regulatory and fiduciary risk.
- **Negotiability:** Low — the Trustee's market position and risk profile make this a "take it or leave it" term.
- **Mitigation in the contract:** Properly limited to "reasonable and documented" expenses; the Trustee must give notice, must obtain Company consent (not unreasonably withheld) for counsel selection and settlement; the Company may participate with its own counsel.

**2. Trustee's Broad Liability Disclaimer (Section 3)**
> Section 3 enumerates a long list of actions for which the Trustee "shall have no responsibility or liability" — including verifying Registration Statement accuracy, Business Combination substance, tax filings, and distribution calculations.

- **Issue:** Combined with the gross negligence / fraud / willful misconduct standard, the Trustee has very limited accountability. The Company relies almost entirely on the Trustee following written instructions in good faith.
- **Why it matters:** If the Trustee makes an error in good faith (e.g., a wire transfer mistake, an incorrect tax calculation), the Company bears the loss.
- **Market standard:** This is **standard** for trustees in this role and is required for the Trustee to take on the engagement.
- **Negotiability:** None — these are industry-standard disclaimers.

**3. No Force Majeure Clause**
- **Issue:** The agreement does not address what happens if performance is impeded by events beyond either party's control (e.g., bank failures, natural disasters, government shutdowns, cyberattacks on Bank of America).
- **Why it matters:** While the Trustee's role is largely passive, in a market disruption or bank failure scenario, there is no contractual framework for extending deadlines, suspending obligations, or re-routing the Trust Account.
- **Market standard:** Many modern agreements include force majeure provisions; however, **absence of force majeure is common in older SPAC trust agreement templates** of this vintage.
- **Negotiability:** Low in this form, but the Company could request addition in future amendments.

**4. No Explicit Dispute Resolution Mechanism Beyond Litigation**
- **Issue:** Disputes go directly to New York state or federal court (Section 6(e)). No mediation, arbitration, or escalation procedure is specified.
- **Why it matters:** Litigation is expensive and time-consuming. The mutual jury trial waiver helps, but the absence of ADR means even minor disputes must go to court.
- **Market standard:** Mixed — many SPAC trust agreements rely on the governing law / venue clause without additional ADR.
- **Negotiability:** Low — adding arbitration would slow enforcement and is not standard.
